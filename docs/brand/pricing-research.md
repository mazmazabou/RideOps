# Campus Transit Dispatch Software — Pricing Research (2026-08-11)

Research basis for RideOps pricing. Two parallel research passes: named competitors, and small-college budget reality.

## What the market charges

**Enterprise campus-transit vendors (TransLoc, DoubleMap/Ride Systems, QRyde, TripShot, Passio, Via):** none publish rate cards — all custom-quoted. TransLoc/DoubleMap/Ride Systems/TapRide are all now one company (Modaxo), which killed the small-campus-friendly options (TapRide retired 2023, forcing campuses like U Michigan onto TransLoc OnDemand). Via's government contracts run **$75k (software-only floor) to $7.9M** (turnkey with vehicles/drivers).

**Best verified anchors:**
| Signal | Amount | Source type |
|---|---|---|

**Small-college reality:** no public record of a sub-6k-student college paying for cart-dispatch software (these deals never hit public procurement). Their transportation offices run low-six-figure total budgets. The failure mode pushing them off phone/radio dispatch is call volume and confusion (SMU's dispatcher: 837 calls in week one), not budget headroom.

## Price bands for a Royal-Ride-scale program

- **No-brainer: $1.5–4k/yr** — at or below Bambi/Joyride small-fleet pricing; trivial vs. any department budget
- **Reasonable, needs justification: $5–12k/yr** — defensible vs. call-volume pain and ADA risk; still 5–10× under Spare-class platforms
- **Out of reach: $15k+/yr** — competes with enterprise paratransit platforms built for regulated transit agencies

## Candidate price points (structure: free 3-month pilot → flat annual per campus)

| Candidate | Annual | Monthly equiv. | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Land the reference** | **$2,900/yr** | ~$240/mo | Deep in the no-brainer band; a Parking Services coordinator can approve without a committee. Optimizes for closing customer #1 and the case study. |
| **B — Fair value (recommended)** | **$4,800/yr** | $400/mo | Top of the no-brainer band. ~10× cheaper than the cheapest verified competitor contract (Spare $53k/yr), above hobby-tool pricing so it reads as a real product. Round, easy to say: "four-eight a year, first three months free." |
| **C — Anchor high** | **$7,500/yr** | $625/mo | Mid defensible band. More credible against enterprise quotes, but likely triggers a procurement/justification process at a small college — slower close. |

All candidates: unlimited riders/drivers/vehicles, all features, email support. No per-ride or per-vehicle meters (differentiator vs. Bambi/Spare, and Royal Ride's fleet is tiny anyway).

**Objection answers ("what does it cost?"):** lead with pilot-free, then the flat annual number, then the competitive frame: "the platforms built for city transit agencies start around $50k/yr — RideOps is built for programs exactly your size."
